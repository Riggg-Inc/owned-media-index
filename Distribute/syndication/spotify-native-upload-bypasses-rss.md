# Spotify Native Upload Bypasses RSS

## What It Is
Platform-native upload requirements for Spotify-first features are causing a divergence from RSS as the single source of truth for owned media programs, raising governance questions about maintaining parity and authority of owned feeds.

## Best For
- Owned media programs relying on RSS as the canonical feed.
- Publishers seeking to leverage Spotify-first streaming/interactive features without losing control of their owned content.

## Why It Works
It acknowledges the evolving platform landscape where some interactive features are platform-native, while emphasizing the need for a deliberate strategy to maintain owned-feed authority and portability. By selectively mirroring content and defining a canonical source, publishers can gain platform reach without full lock-in.

## Required Elements
- Defined canonical source for all owned content (e.g., RSS feed or owned website).
- Clear guidelines for when and what content can be natively uploaded to platforms (e.g., Spotify for interactive features).
- A process for mirroring or linking platform-native content back to the owned canonical source where feasible.
- Ongoing monitoring of platform-exclusive features and their impact on owned media parity.

## Quality Bar
Maintaining RSS feed as the authoritative source for archival and portability, with selective, deliberate native uploads for platform-specific features, including clear content governance.

## When Not To Use
- For content that must remain strictly exclusive to the owned feed without any platform-native distribution.
- Without a clear content governance strategy for handling native-only platform features.
