# New Bliss Closing

## What It Is

A presentation closing structure that does not stop at the call to action. After stating the CTA, the speaker paints the "new bliss" — a vivid picture of what the audience's world looks like once the idea is adopted, including the human flourishing that results.

## Best For

- Keynote and conference talks.
- Executive presentations and sales pitches.
- Persuasive owned-media segments where the goal is adoption or behavior change.
- Webinar and virtual event closings.

## Why It Works

A bare call to action tells the audience what to do but not why the future is worth the effort. Ending on the "new bliss" reframes the ask as a path to a better state, giving the audience an emotional destination attached to the action. Contrasting the current reality with a positive adopted-idea future raises adoption intent by making the payoff concrete and human rather than transactional.

## Required Elements

- A clear, explicit call to action stated first.
- A "new bliss" picture immediately after the CTA.
- Concrete depiction of the adopted-idea world.
- Human flourishing framing, not just feature or outcome lists.
- Emotional contrast between the current state and the new state.

## Quality Bar

The audience should leave able to picture the improved future, not just recall the ask. The closing should feel like an invitation into a better state rather than a request for compliance.

## When Not To Use

- Purely informational or educational content with no persuasive intent.
- Contexts where an aspirational close would read as overselling or manipulative.
- Highly technical audiences expecting a data-only or neutral summary close.
