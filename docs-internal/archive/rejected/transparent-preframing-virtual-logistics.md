# Transparent Preframing for Virtual Logistics

## What It Is

A pattern for presenters in virtual settings to transparently explain their screen setup and potential eye-contact limitations at the start of a session. This proactive communication addresses common audience misinterpretations of presenter focus.

## Best For

- Virtual presentations with live audiences
- Webinars and online workshops
- Hybrid events where a presenter is interacting with both in-room and virtual attendees

## Why It Works

By openly preframing virtual logistics (e.g., "If I look away, I'm checking my notes or engaging with participants on another screen"), presenters can:

- **Maintain audience trust:** Prevents misinterpretation of eye movement as disengagement or distraction.
- **Sustain audience focus:** Reduces cognitive load for attendees who might otherwise be analyzing presenter behavior.
- **Set clear expectations:** Establishes a transparent environment, allowing the audience to concentrate on content.

## Required Elements

- A concise, clear verbal acknowledgment at the beginning of the virtual session.
- Presenter awareness of their virtual setup and potential visual "distractions" from the audience's perspective.

## Quality Bar

The preframing should be:

- **Natural and reassuring:** Avoid sounding apologetic or overly technical.
- **Brief and to the point:** Integrate seamlessly into the introduction without becoming a distraction itself.
- **Audience-centric:** Frame the explanation in terms of enhancing the audience's experience and trust.

## When Not To Use

- In simple virtual setups where the presenter genuinely has no significant eye-contact shifts or external screens that would appear distracting.
- When the primary interaction is audio-only, making visual preframing irrelevant.
