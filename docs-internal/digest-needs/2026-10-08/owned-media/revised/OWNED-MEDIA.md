# What Is Owned Media?

**Published by Riggg Inc.**
**Part of the Owned Media Index — index.riggg.com**

---

This Index distinguishes **owned assets** (content you own or have sufficient rights to use) from **owned channels** (where you control the audience relationship). Social posts and video files can remain assets you control when distributed through rented platforms, subject to the rights you hold; the definition and ownership tests below explain the distinction.

## What Owned Media Means at Riggg

For operating purposes, distinguish content rights from practical control of distribution and audience relationships. Here, “owned assets” is an operational inventory label, not a claim that every item is protected by copyright or that a license transfers ownership. Retain rights, permissions, exports, and migration options; a provider account is not a guarantee of uninterrupted access.

The critical distinction is between the **asset** and the **distribution layer**:

- **The asset** (what you made) is distinct from its distribution platform. Retain masters and confirm ownership or sufficient licenses, including contributor agreements and third-party components. Creating or uploading a file alone does not establish every right. Check the permitted uses, term, territory, and any limits on editing or redistribution in the applicable agreements. Titles and short phrases, for example, are not protected by copyright merely because they appear in this inventory. The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) explains work-made-for-hire and ownership transfers; this is U.S. guidance, not a determination of any particular project’s rights.
- **The distribution layer** (where you put it) affects how much control you have over publishing and audience access. A website and permissioned email list can provide more direct control; retain portable copies and check provider terms. Social-platform reach remains dependent on the platform.

Treat social content as a reusable asset when you hold the necessary rights. That does not give you ownership of the distribution platform.

## The Riggg Framework: 5Ps

Riggg organizes all owned media activity around five operational stages. The 5Ps describe **what you do**. The assets and channels below describe **what you make and where it lives**.

| Stage | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| **Produce** | Capture clean, flexible source media |
| **Package** | Turn source media into distributable assets |
| **Publish** | Get assets into the right channels |
| **Prove** | Measure whether the system worked |
| **Preserve** | Make the content library compound over time |

## Owned Assets by Stage

### Produce

Raw outputs to retain from a recording session, subject to contributor agreements and the rights in incorporated material.

- Video master files
- Audio master files
- Isolated tracks (per speaker)
- Raw footage and B-roll
- Session transcripts (raw)

Assets created to package the source media. Check reuse and distribution rights for each component.

- Episode titles

### Publish

Where the asset goes. This stage splits between **owned channels** (you manage publishing and can maintain a permissioned route to the audience independently of a single provider) and **shared distribution** (access to the platform audience remains platform-dependent). Neither label guarantees uninterrupted service.

#### Owned Channels

Classify these channels by the control and continuity arrangements you actually have, not the product name or domain alone. Retain rights, audience permissions, independent exports, and a tested migration path. A podcast hosting account is supporting infrastructure, not an audience relationship in itself: [Transistor’s terms](https://transistor.fm/terms/) allow suspension or deletion, at its sole discretion and without notice, of accounts it deems inappropriate, offensive, or in violation of its terms. The terms also require upload rights and grant Transistor a non-exclusive license to process user content for operating and maintaining the service. Content rights do not prevent service suspension.

| Channel | What Lives Here |
|---|---|
| **Website** | Canonical episode pages, show page, blog, show notes, transcripts; retain domain control and portable content |
| **Email list / Newsletter** | Episode releases, audience nurture, evergreen sends; retain permission records and usable exports |
| **RSS feed** | Episode distribution from a feed you manage; verify URL control, export and redirect options before relying on host migration |
| **Owned community** | Forum or community with portable content and permissioned member relationships; a custom domain alone does not establish independence |

#### Shared Distribution

Content rights and platform access are separate. A platform change can interrupt reach; independently retained, permissioned audience relationships may remain.

| Platform | Content Type |
|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Full episodes, clips, shorts |
| **Spotify** | Audio and video podcast episodes |
| **Apple Podcasts** | Audio podcast episodes |
| **LinkedIn** | Posts, clips, carousels, newsletters |
| **Instagram** | Reels, clips, quote graphics, stories |
| **X / Twitter** | Posts, threads, clips |
| **Facebook** | Posts, clips, live simulcasts |

!!! note "A note on YouTube"
    YouTube is treated as shared distribution — not owned — for the same reason Spotify and Apple Podcasts are. Google controls the algorithm, monetization rules, and account access. A channel termination or policy change can remove access to that platform audience; independently retained, permissioned relationships may remain. [YouTube’s terms](https://www.youtube.com/static?template=terms) preserve your existing content ownership while requiring upload rights and allowing service changes.

### Prove

Data and reporting assets to retain in your systems, subject to applicable permissions, contracts, and privacy requirements.

- Session performance scorecards
- Per-episode metrics reports
- Benchmark comparisons
- Conversion event logs
- CRM attribution data
- Pipeline influence reports

### Preserve

Assets intended to support long-term retrieval and reuse. AI-search visibility must be measured rather than assumed; [Google’s guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) describes eligibility, not guaranteed inclusion.

- Clean transcripts (indexed and searchable)
- Vector-indexed content library
- Structured show notes for retrieval and reuse
- Schema markup / structured data
- Citation architecture
- Content theme and topic map
- Reuse and repurposing library

## Assets, Channels, and the 5Ps

**Asset rights and distribution control answer different questions.** A social post, clip, or graphic can be a reusable asset when you hold sufficient rights; access to its platform audience remains subject to that platform. A license permits only the uses it covers and does not necessarily make you the copyright owner.

**The 5Ps are the operating model, not a channel-ownership test.** Produce, Package, Publish, Prove, and Preserve describe how Riggg runs the media operation. The asset and channel tests below apply across those stages. They do not promise reach, uninterrupted hosting, or ownership of audience members.

---

## The Ownership Test

When evaluating whether something is owned media, apply two tests:

**Asset test:** Do you own or hold sufficient rights to retain, reuse, and distribute it, including all incorporated components?

**Channel test:** Can you continue publishing and maintain a permissioned route to the audience outside this provider, using retained content, contact or subscription arrangements, and a tested migration path? If yes, treat it as an owned channel for operating purposes, with provider dependencies recorded. If no, treat its audience access as platform-dependent distribution. The answer is specific to the setup; neither exports alone nor a custom domain guarantees continuity.

Build around assets you have rights to use and relationships you can maintain with permission. Test exports and migration options instead of assuming that a platform account guarantees continuity.
