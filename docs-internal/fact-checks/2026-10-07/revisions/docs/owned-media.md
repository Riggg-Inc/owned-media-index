# Owned Media: Definition, Assets, and Channels

This Index distinguishes **owned assets** (the content you create) from **owned channels** (where you control the audience relationship). Social posts and video files can remain assets you control when distributed through rented platforms, subject to the rights you hold; the definition and ownership tests below explain the distinction.

## What Owned Media Means at Riggg

For operating purposes, distinguish content rights from practical control of distribution and audience relationships. Retain rights, permissions, exports, and migration options; a provider account is not a guarantee of uninterrupted access.

The critical distinction is between the **asset** and the **distribution layer**:

- **The asset** (what you made) is distinct from its distribution platform. Retain masters and confirm ownership or sufficient licenses, including contributor agreements and third-party components. Creating or uploading a file alone does not establish every right. The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) explains work-made-for-hire and ownership transfers; this is U.S. guidance, not a determination of any particular project’s rights.
- **The distribution layer** (where you put it) determines whether your *reach* is owned or rented. A website and permissioned email list can provide more direct control; retain portable copies and check provider terms. Social-platform reach remains dependent on the platform.

Treat social content as a reusable asset when you hold the necessary rights. That does not give you ownership of the distribution platform.

## The Riggg Framework: 5Ps

Riggg organizes all owned media activity around five operational stages. The 5Ps describe **what you do**. The assets and channels below describe **what you make and where it lives**.

| Stage | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| **Produce** | Capture clean, flexible source media |
| **Package** | Turn source media into distributable assets |
| **Publish** | Get assets into the right channels |
| **Prove** | Measure whether the system worked |
| **Preserve** | Make the content library compound over time |

## Owned Assets by Stage

Raw outputs to retain from a recording session, subject to contributor agreements and the rights in incorporated material.

- Audio master files
- Isolated tracks (per speaker)
- Raw footage and B-roll
- Session transcripts (raw)

### Publish

Where the asset goes. This stage splits between **owned channels** (you control the infrastructure and the audience relationship) and **shared distribution** (you retain content rights, not ownership of the platform).

#### Owned Channels

These channels can improve continuity when you retain rights, audience permissions, independent exports, and a migration path. A hosting account still depends on its provider: [Transistor’s terms](https://transistor.fm/terms/) reserve account suspension or deletion.

| Channel | What Lives Here |
|---|---|
| **Website** | Canonical episode pages, show page, blog, show notes, transcripts |
| **Email list / Newsletter** | Episode releases, audience nurture, evergreen sends |
| **RSS feed** | The authoritative feed origin you control (via Buzzsprout, Transistor, etc.) |
| **Podcast hosting account** | Feed management, analytics, distribution control |
| **Owned community** | Forum or community platform hosted on your own domain |

#### Shared Distribution

Content rights and platform access are separate. A platform change can interrupt reach; independently retained, permissioned audience relationships may remain.

| Platform | Content Type |
|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Full episodes, clips, shorts |
| **Spotify** | Audio and video podcast episodes |
| **Apple Podcasts** | Audio podcast episodes |
| **LinkedIn** | Posts, clips, carousels, newsletters |
| **Instagram** | Reels, clips, quote graphics, stories |
| **X / Twitter** | Posts, threads, clips |
| **Facebook** | Posts, clips, live simulcasts |

!!! note "A note on YouTube"
    YouTube is treated as shared distribution — not owned — for the same reason Spotify and Apple Podcasts are. Google controls the algorithm, monetization rules, and account access. A channel termination or policy change can remove access to that platform audience; independently retained, permissioned relationships may remain. [YouTube’s terms](https://www.youtube.com/static?template=terms) preserve your existing content ownership while requiring upload rights and allowing service changes.

### Prove

Data and reporting assets to retain in your systems, subject to applicable permissions, contracts, and privacy requirements.

- Session performance scorecards
- Per-episode metrics reports
- Benchmark comparisons
- Conversion event logs
- CRM attribution data
- Pipeline influence reports

### Preserve

Assets intended to support long-term retrieval and reuse. AI-search visibility must be measured rather than assumed; [Google’s guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) describes eligibility, not guaranteed inclusion.

- Clean transcripts (indexed and searchable)
- Vector-indexed content library
- Structured show notes optimized for LLMs
- Schema markup / structured data
- Citation architecture
- Content theme and topic map
- Reuse and repurposing library

## How This Relates to PESO

The PESO Model (Dietrich, 2014) classifies media as Paid, Earned, Shared, and Owned. Riggg aligns with the spirit of that model but sharpens two points:

**1. Social content is owned; social platforms are shared.**
Gini Dietrich places social media in the Shared quadrant. At Riggg, we split the asset from the distribution layer. The post you write, the clip you cut, the graphic you design — those are owned media. The platform you post them to is the shared layer. Check the rights in what you create separately from the conditions governing its distribution.

**2. The 5Ps replace the channel taxonomy as the operating model.**
PESO helps you categorize media types. The Riggg 5Ps describe how to run a media operation — Produce, Package, Publish, Prove, Preserve. Where PESO asks "what kind of media is this?", the OMI asks "what stage of the system are we in?"

## The Ownership Test

When evaluating whether something is owned media, apply two tests:

**Asset test:** Do you own or hold sufficient rights to retain, reuse, and distribute it, including all incorporated components?

**Channel test:** If the platform shut down tomorrow, would you lose the audience relationship? → If yes, it's shared distribution, not an owned channel.

Build around assets you have rights to use and relationships you can maintain with permission. Test exports and migration options instead of assuming that a platform account guarantees continuity.
