# Owned Media: Definition, Assets, and Channels

Owned media is the content you own and the channels where you manage publishing and maintain a direct audience relationship. Keep the distinction simple: **the asset is what you made; distribution is where it reaches people.**

## What Owned Media Means at Riggg

A video master, transcript, clip or written post can be an owned asset even when you publish it on a rented platform. Posting your video on YouTube does not make YouTube yours—or stop the video from being yours.

- **Owned assets:** content your organization owns and can reuse. Check that you have the rights needed to use contributor material, music and other third-party content. Licensed material is usable within its licence, not necessarily owned.
- **Owned channels:** your website, email list and managed RSS feed give you more direct control over publishing and audience access than a social account. Keep content backups, subscriber exports and permission records, and know how to move providers.
- **Shared distribution:** LinkedIn, YouTube and other platforms help your content reach people, but control access to their own audiences.

Start with one episode: keep the master and transcript, publish an episode page on your website, then distribute clips through social channels. You own the assets; you do not own the pipe.

## The Riggg Framework: 5Ps

Riggg organizes all owned media activity around five operational stages. The 5Ps describe **what you do**. The assets and channels below describe **what you make and where it lives**.

| Stage | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| **Produce** | Capture clean, flexible source media |
| **Package** | Turn source media into distributable assets |
| **Publish** | Get assets into the right channels |
| **Prove** | Measure whether the system worked |
| **Preserve** | Make the content library compound over time |

## Owned Assets by Stage

### Produce

Source assets to keep from each recording session.

- Video master files
- Audio master files
- Isolated tracks (per speaker)
- Raw footage and B-roll
- Session transcripts (raw)

### Publish

Where the asset goes: use **owned channels** as your publishing home and **shared distribution** to extend its reach.

#### Owned Channels

Build these so you can keep publishing and stay in touch when you change providers. A website or list still needs hosting; backups and exports make it easier to move.

| Channel | What Lives Here |
|---|---|
| **Website** | Canonical episode pages, show page, blog, show notes, transcripts |
| **Email list / Newsletter** | Episode releases, audience nurture, evergreen sends |
| **RSS feed** | The feed you manage; check export and redirect options before changing hosts |
| **Owned community** | Forum or community with content and member relationships you can carry to another provider |

Podcast hosts such as Buzzsprout and Transistor provide the infrastructure for feed management, analytics and distribution. Keep copies of your files and check the host’s migration options; the account itself is not an audience relationship.

#### Shared Distribution

You own your content, not the platform or its algorithm. A shutdown or rule change can cut off that platform’s reach; your website and email list give people another way to find you.

| Platform | Content Type |
|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Full episodes, clips, shorts |
| **Spotify** | Audio and video podcast episodes |
| **Apple Podcasts** | Audio podcast episodes |
| **LinkedIn** | Posts, clips, carousels, newsletters |
| **Instagram** | Reels, clips, quote graphics, stories |
| **X / Twitter** | Posts, threads, clips |
| **Facebook** | Posts, clips, live simulcasts |

!!! note "A note on YouTube"
    YouTube is treated as shared distribution — not owned — for the same reason Spotify and Apple Podcasts are. Google controls the algorithm, monetization rules, and account access. A channel termination can cut off access to that YouTube audience. Keep the video masters and give viewers a way to find you outside YouTube.

### Prove

Reports and data you retain to understand what worked. Keep customer information private and respect data-use permissions.

- Session performance scorecards
- Per-episode metrics reports
- Benchmark comparisons
- Conversion event logs
- CRM attribution data
- Pipeline influence reports

---

### Preserve

Keep these assets organized so your team can find, cite and reuse what it already knows.

- Clean transcripts (indexed and searchable)
- Vector-indexed content library
- Structured show notes for retrieval and reuse
- Schema markup / structured data
- Citation architecture
- Content theme and topic map
- Reuse and repurposing library

## How This Relates to PESO

The [PESO Model](https://spinsucks.com/peso-model/), created by Gini Dietrich and introduced in 2014, connects Paid, Earned, Shared and Owned media in an integrated marketing and communications system.

Riggg’s asset-versus-distribution distinction helps you plan what to keep and where to publish: the clip is an asset; the social platform is its shared distribution channel. The **5Ps** organize the work of producing, packaging, publishing, proving and preserving those assets. Use PESO to plan the media mix and the 5Ps to run the production workflow.

## The Ownership Test

Use two checks when planning your next release:

- **Asset check:** Do we own the content, and have the rights needed to publish and reuse what is in it? Keep the master files somewhere the team can find them.
- **Channel check:** If we change providers, can we take our content and keep reaching subscribers? Try a content backup or subscriber export, keep permission records, and check the feed redirect process.

Build around assets you can reuse and relationships you can maintain. Rented reach is valuable—just do not make it your only route to the audience.
