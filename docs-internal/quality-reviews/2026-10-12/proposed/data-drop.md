# Data Drop

A clip built around a specific statistic, benchmark, or data point that surprises or reframes the audience's understanding. Choose a credible, surprising number the speaker explains; avoid statistics that lose their meaning without extensive context.

**Stage:** Package → Clips  
**Score:** 4  
**Evidence:** internal production data

## What It Is

A clip built around a specific statistic, benchmark, or data point that surprises or reframes the audience's understanding.

## Why It Works

A well-sourced statistic can give the audience a concrete reference point and help explain why a topic matters. Preserve the source, population, timeframe and qualifications that make the number meaningful. Test whether the resulting clip helps your audience rather than assuming data alone creates authority or improves performance.

## Selection Criteria

- Statistic is surprising or counterintuitive
- Source is credible and recent
- Data point reframes how the audience thinks about the topic
- Speaker contextualizes the number — not just reads it

## When Not To Use

Avoid when the statistic is well-known, when the source is questionable, or when the number requires heavy context to be meaningful.

## Prompt Template

Copy and customize this prompt to generate this pattern:

```
Identify and extract a Data Drop clip moment from this transcript. Look for a surprising statistic, benchmark, or data point that reframes understanding.

Transcript excerpt:
[paste transcript section]

Requirements:
- Statistic is surprising or counterintuitive
- Preserve the statistic exactly, including population, timeframe and qualifications
- List the source supplied in the transcript; if absent or unverified, flag it for the editor rather than inventing one
- Speaker contextualizes the number
- Aim for 30-60 seconds when spoken, but keep essential context
- Provide start/end timestamps only when supplied; otherwise quote opening/closing words and say timing needs verification
- Provide the data point, necessary context, source-check questions and a suggested clip title
- If no suitable moment exists, say so; do not manufacture a number or claim
```

Replace the bracketed placeholders with your specific details. The more context you provide about your audience, guest, and episode content, the better the output.
