## usefulness — needs_revision
The destination/aspect-ratio tables and editor presets answer a real export task, but old maximum durations and account-independent limits can cause avoidable delivery decisions; the reference cannot pass on usefulness merely because it is detailed.
## substance — pass
Horizontal, vertical, audio and image settings plus Resolve/Premiere presets are concrete and substantial. Preserve those examples and do not replace the whole reference with a generic verify-everything disclaimer.
## coherence — needs_revision
The opening tells readers to distinguish workflow presets from upload limits, but the body still mixes fixed 30fps/bitrate recommendations with Max Duration/Max Size requirements and promises the first export meets platform requirements. The distinction needs to exist in the tables, not just the opening caveat.
## cross_page_consistency — pass
Audio targets agree with the neighboring Audio Mastering working presets; Video Mastering delegates destination settings here. That dependency makes the reference accuracy material, while shared numbers across siblings are not independent evidence.
## reasonable_claims — needs_revision
Platform maxima require dated primary-source checks and account/upload-route qualifiers. The unqualified Reels/TikTok/LinkedIn limits and universal first-export promise are not made reliable by the May snapshot footnote; supported YouTube Shorts detail should remain.
## usability — needs_revision
Light desktop tables and presets are understandable, but mobile dark alternating rows and code are materially unreadable. Wide tables use constrained horizontal regions rather than whole-page overflow; this does not cure low contrast in required cells.
