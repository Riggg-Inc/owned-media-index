# Build an Audience Question Set for AI Discovery Checks

**Stage:** Preserve
**Score:** 3
**Evidence:** Practitioner Observation

Start with questions your audience actually asks, use owned interviews and source material to answer them substantively, then test a small fixed sample in AI assistants. Unbranded questions test discovery; branded questions test accuracy. Neither a well-written answer nor a favorable test guarantees future AI inclusion.

## What It Is

A practical workflow connecting audience questions, owned-media answers and repeatable AI-answer observations. The question set is an editorial research tool first and a discovery check second—not a list of keywords to stuff into a transcript or dozens of near-identical FAQ pages.

The owned asset is the interview, transcript or written answer you create. Your canonical website page is the durable reference you control; an AI assistant is a distribution surface you do not control.

## Best For

- Teams with a defined audience and access to permissioned sales questions, customer conversations, recorded interviews or on-site search logs.
- Editors deciding which questions a recorded series should explore and which existing answers need clarification.
- Programs that can maintain a small observation log without confusing it with a market-wide visibility score.

## Why It Works

The practical value is traceability: you can explain why a question matters, show where an answer came from and identify what is still missing. Starting with audience language reduces the temptation to test only prompts that describe your company perfectly. Asking subject-matter experts for examples, limits and counterexamples makes an interview more useful than repeating a marketing claim.

These are practitioner judgments about editorial quality and test design, not measured proof that this workflow increases AI citations. A readable, accessible answer can be useful to people and available for discovery without being selected by an engine. SEO fundamentals still matter; AI checks complement rather than replace search, audience and conversion reporting.

## Required Elements

### 1. Build a question register from audience evidence

Review a bounded recent sample: sales objections and lost-deal questions, support tickets, customer research, approved interview transcripts and site-search queries. Keep the collection window and source coverage visible. These sources are imperfect: sales notes represent buyers who contacted you, and site search represents people already on your site. Neither establishes total market demand.

For each candidate, record:

- A stable question ID and the original wording in an access-controlled register.
- Source type, date and a permissioned reference or timestamp—not customer details in a public article or AI prompt.
- Audience role, decision being made and actual constraints such as budget, team capacity, timing, geography or compliance.
- The safe public phrasing, with any anonymization or wording change explained.
- Why it belongs in the set: repeated concern within the reviewed sample, a consequential blocker, a misunderstood tradeoff or an unanswered question. Record sample counts only if actually counted.
- Whether it is observed audience language or an editorial hypothesis awaiting validation.

Group close duplicates without erasing meaningful differences. “Can we do this with one marketer?” is not interchangeable with “What does a large production team need?” Do not replace a buyer’s plain wording with your positioning language. Ask a salesperson or interviewer what the person was trying to decide when a fragment is ambiguous.

### 2. Cover the decision, not just the vendor shortlist

Choose a manageable starting panel—for example, six to ten questions across the decision types below. This size is a workload suggestion, not a statistically representative sample. Prioritize relevance and unanswered decisions over an arbitrary quota.

**All examples below are hypothetical B2B podcast-buyer questions, not customer quotes, observed demand or tested AI results.**

| Type | Example unbranded question | What a useful owned answer needs |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | How can a two-person B2B marketing team turn expert knowledge into useful content without booking the founder every week? | Diagnose the time bottleneck; compare interviews with other ways to capture expertise; explain when a podcast is unnecessary. |
| Approach | Should our B2B expert-interview series start audio-only or include video if we have one marketer and no editor? | Describe the production tasks, quality tradeoffs, capacity assumptions and a feasible pilot—not “video always wins.” |
| Selection | What should we ask a podcast production partner before signing if our team still needs to approve every episode? | An approval workflow, responsibilities, revision limits, rights, deliverables and questions to verify in a proposal. |
| Recommendation | Which B2B podcast production providers should we evaluate for remote interviews when we need editing and clips but will handle guest booking ourselves? | Transparent fit criteria and verifiable service scope; acknowledge missing budget/location information and cases where an in-house workflow fits better. |

An educational answer need not mention your company. A strong answer may appropriately cite independent research, a platform manual or another expert. A provider recommendation is a different task from explaining a production tradeoff; do not grade them against the same brand-mention expectation.

### 3. Map questions to substantive owned answers

For each question, identify an existing canonical answer, supporting interview/transcript passages and evidence gaps. If several pages answer the same question, improve one canonical resource and link relevant episodes to it rather than publishing competing thin variations.

Turn gaps into an interview brief. Ask the guest: What would you do under these constraints? What steps are involved? What failed? What would change your recommendation? Which example or source supports that claim? A confident guest assertion is still an assertion until supported. Preserve the context, speaker attribution and timestamps; obtain publication permission and remove confidential details.

Publish an answer a reader can actually use:

- Open with the direct answer and the conditions that change it.
- Explain the reasoning, steps, tradeoffs and limits.
- Include a worked example, checklist or decision criteria that goes beyond restating the question.
- Link supporting public evidence near the claim, and the relevant approved transcript or recording passage where available. Distinguish measured results, expert opinion and hypothetical examples.
- Answer likely related followups, or link to a substantive related resource. Use clear headings and ordinary readable text, not hidden answers or keyword repetition.
- Assign an owner to correct outdated facts and confirm that the page and its supporting links are publicly accessible where intended.

**Hypothetical answer example — audio or video:** “For a team with one marketer and no editor, start with an audio pilot unless showing the work is essential to the audience’s understanding and someone can own video production. Before choosing video, name who will manage camera setup, visual editing, graphics and approvals. If no one owns those tasks, outsourcing or a simpler format is more realistic than adding an unattended workload.”

That is a starting answer, not a universal rule. A substantive page would then show a sample responsibility map, compare audio/video tasks, link a relevant production walkthrough and explain exceptions such as product demonstrations. Interview followups could ask the producer what can be simplified, what cannot be skipped and how the team will judge the pilot. Do not invent hours saved, conversion results or case studies to make the answer seem stronger.

### 4. Freeze a small prompt set before testing

For each baseline prompt, save the exact text, question ID, provenance/rationale, audience constraints, decision type, intended answer page and version. Write it as the audience would ask it. Add only context supported by your question research; do not engineer a combination of attributes that uniquely points to your company.

Keep two separate panels:

- **Unbranded discovery:** no company name, domain or instruction to include your company. Observe whether the response surfaces relevant providers or resources without being led.
- **Branded accuracy:** explicitly ask about your company, such as “What services does [Company] provide for a B2B interview podcast, and what would our team still handle?” Compare the answer with dated, public service information. This tests representation, not spontaneous discovery.

Do not silently rephrase a prompt until a favorable mention appears. If new audience evidence warrants a change, version it, explain why and start a separate comparison series. Retain the previous observations, including absences and errors.

### 5. Separate baseline runs from realistic conversations

Run each baseline prompt in a fresh chat without earlier hints about your company. Record engine/product, model if shown, UTC date/time, search or browsing mode, language, relevant location and login/personalization context. A fresh chat does not necessarily remove account-level personalization; use available controls and document what you cannot control.

Save the exact prompt, response or permitted screenshot/export, cited URLs and run ID. If the engine is unavailable, log “not tested,” not “no visibility.” Use a small predeclared repeat count, such as two runs per prompt per review, if capacity permits. Keep all runs rather than the best screenshot. Comparisons are more interpretable when the engine, mode and prompts remain consistent.

Run realistic followups separately. For example, the hypothetical buyer might add “We can record only once a month; how would that change the plan?” Preserve the full conversation chain and distinguish audience-derived followups from editor-invented ones. Do not combine these results with fresh-chat baselines or add your company’s name midway and call the result unbranded discovery.

### 6. Observe mentions, citations and representation separately

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Mention | Did the answer name the company or asset? Record the actual wording and role; a passing mention is not a recommendation. |
| Citation | Was there a source link to your canonical page, a third-party page about you or neither? Save the exact URL and the claim it is attached to. Open it to check that it supports the claim. |
| Accurate representation | Check specific claims about services, scope, facts or advice against current public evidence. Mark correct, incorrect or unverifiable and explain; a citation does not automatically make the answer accurate. |
| Other useful sources | Note research, competitors, manuals or experts that answer the question well, without treating every non-brand source as a failure. |
| Answer gap | What relevant constraint, evidence or followup was missed? Is the gap in your content, the response or both? |

Report the denominator and context: “Named in 3 of 16 fresh-chat runs of this fixed panel on these engines and dates” is a sample description, not a claim about all buyers. These observations are not search volume, market share, universal AI visibility or conversions. A branded mention is not evidence of discovery; absence in this panel is not evidence of universal invisibility.

## Example Patterns

**From sales objection to interview and answer.** A hypothetical question about episode approvals becomes an interview with a producer about responsibility, review deadlines and revision limits. The canonical answer includes a sample handoff checklist; the transcript supplies an attributed explanation. The unbranded prompt asks what to check before hiring, not “Why is our agency’s process best?”

**From site-search gap to clarification.** A hypothetical “who owns podcast files” search leads an editor to inspect whether the service and episode pages explain masters, transcripts, licensing and delivery. A new answer distinguishes ownership of created assets from access to a rented platform. A branded accuracy check can then reveal whether an assistant describes the public policy correctly; it does not prove the page caused a change.

**From useful non-brand result to learning.** An engine cites independent research for an educational question. The editor checks whether the source answers the audience’s real concern, then improves the owned answer’s explanation or sourcing if needed. There is no requirement to force a company mention into an answer where it adds nothing.

## Quality Bar

- Every retained question has traceable audience evidence or a clearly labeled hypothesis.
- Audience wording and material constraints survive anonymization. Public prompts contain no confidential or identifying customer details.
- Each priority question maps to a substantive canonical answer or an explicit content gap, with interview/source references and an accountable editor.
- Examples and evidence limits remain visible; FAQ formatting never substitutes for actual expertise.
- The frozen prompts, baseline/followup separation, exact responses and test context make observations auditable.
- A favorable citation is not the acceptance criterion. Useful, supportable answers and honest observations are.

Use a learning loop: question evidence → interview/source review → canonical answer → sampled checks → correction or further research. Prioritize factual errors and missing audience decisions before cosmetic wording changes. Rerun the fixed panel after a documented update at your next planned review; record other changes that may affect comparison. A different result after an edit is not proof that the edit caused it. Publishing more often, joining a particular third-party platform or adding schema is not a guarantee of citation.

## When Not To Use

- Do not use this panel to estimate total demand, declare market leadership or promise AI rankings.
- Do not manufacture an audience from competitor prompts when you have not validated the underlying questions. Begin with audience research instead.
- Do not upload private sales notes or customer transcripts to an external assistant without permission and appropriate data controls.
- Do not produce mass FAQ variants, fabricated interviews or unsupported answers just to fill the question map.
- Do not let monitoring displace maintaining useful content or measuring actual audience outcomes. Search performance, referral visits and qualified inquiries remain separate measures.

## Evidence

Evidence level: practitioner-observation

**Score 3 — Viable, capped at the practitioner-observation level.** This is editorial and measurement guidance based on operator judgment, not a benchmark-proven intervention. Its usefulness depends on the quality and coverage of audience research, access to subject-matter expertise and careful interpretation of a small sample. The hypothetical B2B examples demonstrate the procedure; they are not evidence of customer outcomes or AI behavior.

No controlled evaluation is offered showing that this workflow increases mentions, citations or inquiries. Prior citation-share, ranking-overlap and publishing-cadence claims are not evidence for this intervention and are not carried forward. The procedure makes uncertainty and content gaps inspectable; it does not guarantee AI inclusion.
