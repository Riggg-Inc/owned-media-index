# Distribution Platforms Quadrant

Build on channels you own: your website, email list, podcast (through its RSS feed), webinars and owned community. Use shared platforms to get found, then do the work of bringing those people into what you own. This page covers both owned channels and shared platforms. Owning your content files is not the same as owning the audience relationship.

## How To Read This

**Top-left (Owned + Low discovery):** This is where you start. You control these, but at the beginning their reach is small and you have to grow it. *Email*, *Website*, *Podcast*, *Webinars* and *Owned community* live here. Your podcast's RSS feed is its canonical access point, like your website domain. Most people use a podcast hosting provider rather than running a feed themselves, so choose one that lets you leave: you should be able to set a 301 redirect from the old feed address and take your show and subscribers with you. That portability is the part you own.

**Top-right (Owned + High discovery):** This is the goal. Nothing starts here. You get there by growing the owned channels in the top-left until they reach a large audience that you still control.

**Bottom-right (Shared + High discovery):** The reach is real, but you're a tenant. *YouTube*, *LinkedIn*, *Instagram*, *TikTok*, *X / Twitter* and *Facebook* have large audiences, but the platform owns the relationship, and algorithm changes can crater your numbers overnight. Use them to get found, then bring people into your email list, website or RSS feed. YouTube is now the most-used podcast platform in the US, so a video version of your show belongs here too. It's still discovery, not ownership.

**Bottom-left (Shared + Low discovery):** Apple Podcasts and Spotify list your RSS feed. Both have some discovery features, but the listener relationship belongs to the app, and most growth still comes from audiences you bring in from elsewhere. Reddit is useful for community participation where subreddit rules allow it. None of these is a foundation.

## The Ownership Gradient

The vertical axis is the strategic insight. What you own with a podcast is its RSS feed address and the ability to move it. Shared platforms are different: you can use them well, but you can't take their audience with you. Build around what you control and use platforms to get found.

| Strategy | Example |
|---|---|
| Build owned, distribute shared | Publish to your site and email first, then syndicate to YouTube and LinkedIn |
| Use shared distribution to capture an owned audience | Instagram Reels → Newsletter signup → Email relationship. The capture step is work you have to do; the platform won't do it for you |
| Keep one canonical RSS feed | Choose a host that supports 301 redirects and full export; submit that one feed everywhere |
| Never single-platform dependent | If YouTube is your only channel, you have a YouTube problem, not a distribution strategy |

## Platform Notes

### Anchors: owned channels you build on
- **Email / Newsletter** — Keep an exportable subscriber list with permission records. Deliverability and provider rules still affect inbox access.
- **Website / Blog** — Your canonical home. Keep backups and domain access, and know how to move hosting providers.
- **Podcast (RSS feed)** — Your show's canonical access point. You own the feed address, not the listener identities. See Top-left for portability.
- **Webinars** — Live or recorded sessions you run yourself, with registrations and attendee lists you can export. Strong for lead capture; reach starts small. Ownership depends on the tool: make sure you can export registrants and recordings.
- **Owned community** — A forum or community whose content and member relationships you can carry to another provider. Ownership depends on the tool: a Discord server or Facebook Group is shared, not owned.

### Hybrid
- **Substack** — Part email newsletter and podcast manager, part platform with social features. Owned only to the extent you can export your list and take your content with you, so it's less owned than your own email or website.

### Amplifiers: shared platforms used to get found
- **YouTube** — The biggest source of reach, including for podcasts: it is the most-used podcast platform in the US. Subscriber notifications give platform-mediated access, not audience ownership. You're one policy change from trouble.
- **LinkedIn** — LinkedIn controls the audience, the feed and the rules, so followers are not an owned audience. Useful for professional distribution when your audience is active there; bring people into your email list, website or RSS.
- **Instagram** — Visual-first discovery, strong for clips and quote graphics. You don't own the follower relationship.
- **TikTok** — High short-form discovery when your audience is there. Least control of any platform on the chart.
- **X / Twitter** — Real-time reach exists but is volatile. Algorithm and platform stability are wildcards.
- **Facebook** — Groups and pages can still reach niche audiences. Organic reach varies widely, so test before you invest.
- **Reddit** — Participate in relevant communities where the rules allow it. The relationship stays with Reddit.

### Directories: list your RSS feed and add some discovery
- **Spotify** — Lists your RSS feed and recommends podcasts on its Home feed. Real discovery, but Spotify controls the follower relationship.
- **Apple Podcasts** — Directory listing with charts and editorial features. Still a large listening app, but it rarely drives growth on its own.

## Choosing where to publish

| Goal | Start with | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term audience you control | Email, Website, Podcast (RSS), Webinars, Owned community | Reach starts small. Plan how you'll feed them. |
| Maximum discovery | YouTube, then the short-form platform where your audience is | You don't own the followers. Always route people to an Anchor. |
| B2B / professional audience | LinkedIn, plus Email | LinkedIn controls the feed. Capture people into email. |
| Podcast distribution | One portable RSS feed, submitted to Apple, Spotify and others, plus a YouTube version | Directories rarely drive growth on their own. |
| Short-form clips | YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok | Clips are for discovery. Every clip should point somewhere you own. |
| Written companion | Email + Website (Substack if you want its network) | Substack is a hybrid. Confirm you can export your list. |
| Community engagement | Owned community, plus Reddit / LinkedIn / Facebook Groups where your audience already is | Communities on shared platforms are not owned. |
