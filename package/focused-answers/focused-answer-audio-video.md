# Focused Answer Audio and Video

## What It Is

A separately titled audio recording or topic video that answers one specific audience question completely. Give it a stable page on your own site with a corrected transcript and useful source links, so people can find the answer again without searching through a full episode.

Start by writing: **“This is for ___, who wants to know ___. Afterwards, they can ___.”** Record the answer deliberately, or extract it faithfully from a longer recording. Make it complete—not arbitrarily short.

## Best For

- Recurring questions that deserve an explanation rather than a teaser.
- Expert answers buried inside longer interviews.
- Screen demonstrations or decisions that need an example but not a whole episode.
- A library of answers people can enter through the question they have today.

## Why It Works

A clear question gives the asset a useful boundary: include what helps someone resolve it and leave unrelated discussion elsewhere. A specific title and opening make the promise easy to recognize; a worked example makes the answer easier to use.

The answer can be the destination. Related episodes offer more depth, but the audience should not need to follow a chain of clips to receive what the title promised. A focused answer can also belong to a series: state any prerequisite instead of pretending every topic starts from zero.

## Required Elements

- **One question and a finish line.** Identify the intended audience, what they already need to know, and what they should be able to explain, do or decide afterwards.
- **The right medium.** Use audio for an explanation that makes sense without a screen. Use video when the answer depends on a visible procedure, diagram or comparison. “Click here” is not an audio instruction.
- **A complete answer.** Include the direct answer, necessary assumptions, a concrete example, the exception that changes the advice, and a way to check the result. Set this content budget before choosing a runtime.
- **An honest promise.** The title, thumbnail and opening describe the same question. A broad promise such as “Master podcast production” does not fit one microphone check.
- **Context-preserving edits.** Keep the original question, speaker attribution, relevant date or version, and qualifications that affect meaning. If an extract cannot stand alone, add clearly distinguished context or make a fresh recording; do not stitch the speaker into answering a different question.
- **An owned home.** Publish the approved recording with a corrected transcript and sources on a stable page. Keep the media, captions and metadata available for future edits and reuse.
- **Optional next steps.** Chapters, related answers and a link to the full conversation help navigation. They must not conceal an unfinished answer.
- **Permission for the intended use.** Check the edit and destination against the permissions for the recording and any included music, images or screens.

**Illustrative example:** Answer “How do I check that my interview is recording the right microphone?” Show the selected input, record a short test, play it back, and explain what to check if it is silent or using the laptop microphone. Finish with the viewer able to verify the sound—not merely find the settings menu. Name the recording application and version. Use a screen video when the controls are essential; an audio version needs instructions that work without seeing them.

## Quality Bar

- Give the recording to someone who fits the intended audience but has not seen the source episode. Can they explain the answer or complete the promised task without asking for missing steps?
- Compare every extract with its original passage. The question, attribution and meaning-changing caveats still match.
- Listen without the screen if it is offered as audio. Watch on the intended device if visual detail carries the answer.
- Check the title against the finished recording. Remove promises the answer does not fulfill.
- Read the transcript, open the source links and test playback on the owned page.
- Ask whether the question was answered. Do not treat completion percentage as proof of understanding: someone may replay a step or leave once they have what they need.

## When Not To Use

Keep a longer, chaptered recording when the explanation depends on several interacting assumptions, a nuanced disagreement or a story whose setup changes its meaning. Do not cut to a target length by removing necessary reasoning or speeding through essential steps.

If a chapter link already provides a complete, easy-to-find answer, a separate recording may not earn its production cost. Use Clip Selection Patterns when the job is simply to choose a useful excerpt, rather than design a standalone answer.

3

Viable when a question has a clear boundary and the team can preserve enough context. Producing and maintaining separate answers takes judgment and may cost more than linking to a chapter.

## Evidence

Evidence level: practitioner-observation.

The complete-answer checklist is an editorial recommendation, illustrated by public creator work—not a tested performance formula. It does not establish an ideal duration or improved learning, audience retention or business results.
