# Put Your Best Explanations Where People Need Them

## What It Is

Give someone an existing, approved explanation when they ask the question it answers. Use a customer interview passage, expert answer or implementation walkthrough with readable supporting text on a stable page you control. The conversation, help reply or partner page is the route; your owned page remains the home.

Start with one recurring question and one recording that answers it—not a new distribution platform.

**Illustrative example:** A buyer asks, “How much work would our team handle during rollout?” You already have a released customer interview describing that customer's responsibilities. Answer directly, then offer the relevant passage and transcript: “This customer explains what their team handled and what the provider handled. You can read the same section below the video.” Identify differences from the buyer's situation rather than implying an identical rollout.

## Best For

- Sales and customer-success teams answering recurring questions with an approved recording library.
- Buyers who need to forward a useful explanation to colleagues who missed the conversation.
- Support teams connecting a requested answer to a maintained walkthrough or checklist.
- Programs with useful owned episode pages but few deliberate routes from everyday questions to those pages.

## Why It Works

A question supplies the reason to share. Instead of sending someone a whole episode and asking them to find the useful part, point them to the explanation they need, with enough written context to act without watching.

A stable owned page also gives the next reader somewhere to check the source, date and qualifications. That matters when a buyer forwards your answer without you there to explain it.

These are practical reasons to try the method. Published cases document reusable demo libraries and buyer-group sharing; they do not establish that this approach improves retention, sales results or viewing behavior.

## Required Elements

### Choose the answer before the route

Write down who asks the question, what they need to understand and which approved asset answers it. Read or watch the full relevant source passage, including its surrounding question and qualifications. A keyword match is not enough.

Check the recording date, current product or service details, and permission for the intended use. Keep internal-only material separate from externally shareable assets. A recorded customer or prospect conversation is not automatically a marketing asset.

Use the [Owned Media Library](../../preserve/vector-memory/content-memory-standard.md) for source custody and clearance, and the [Canonical Episode Page](../website/canonical-episode-page.md) for the destination. The written answer should stand alone; playback is optional.

### Make buying conversations easier to follow

The following are **proposed applications**, not reported customer deployments:

- **Explain implementation.** A project lead asks what must be connected, tested and signed off. Offer a current recorded walkthrough with a checklist separating the buyer's responsibilities from the provider's. Link to the relevant section, not a generic homepage.
- **Help a buyer brief a colleague.** An internal sponsor asks for an access-control explanation to forward to IT. Supply a cleared expert answer with speaker role, recording or review date, applicable version, important limits and links to current formal documentation. Remove “as we discussed” and confidential deal context. Restricted details need an access-controlled destination and a preview that does not reveal them.
- **Prepare from the approved library.** Before a conversation in an unfamiliar sector, search released expert interviews and customer stories for relevant workflow concerns. Open the source and listen in context. Use it to inform questions, not to treat one person's experience as a fact about an entire sector. An AI summary can suggest a passage; a person must verify the source and sharing permission.
- **Show a requested example.** When a buyer asks what a finished deliverable looks like, link a cleared example beside the relevant proposal section. Explain what it demonstrates and where the proposed scope differs. The proposal must remain understandable without playback.

Start with an ordinary human-written reply. A new sales-room platform or automated outreach sequence is not required.

### Use the same principle outside sales

These are also **ideas to test**, not measured success stories:

- Answer a support question in text, then offer the exact section of a maintained recording-derived guide.
- Put a microphone setup recording and text checklist behind a clearly labeled QR code **and** short readable URL on a recording-kit card. Keep essential instructions available without scanning.
- Place an optional example beside a confusing form field, such as what to include in an audio-problem sample. Do not require watching before submission.
- With an existing partner editor's permission, link a joint setup walkthrough from the relevant help page. Agree who will update or remove the link when instructions change.

### Send enough context to make the link useful

Use this reply template after checking the actual source:

> You asked: [specific question].
>
> The answer is: [complete, checked answer in plain language].
>
> For the example or walkthrough: [descriptive link to the exact owned-page section].
>
> This applies to [version or situation]. [Important difference or limit, if needed].

A person should choose whether the resource fits each request. Do not insert promotional links automatically into conversations or turn a service reply into newsletter enrollment.

### Check whether it helped

Try one cleared asset in one appropriate type of requested reply. Keep a simple aggregate record of offers, voluntary responses, responses saying it helped, and broken or outdated links. For example, if 10 people receive the link, four reply and three say it helped, report **3 of 4 respondents, from 10 offers**—not “75% of recipients found it useful.” These numbers illustrate reporting, not a target or result.

Ask what remained unanswered. Check that a colleague can find the intended passage and that a forwarded link still makes sense. Use only authorized, consent-compatible measurement; a visit is not buying intent. Assign someone to maintain the destination and replace or withdraw stale guidance.

## Quality Bar

Before offering the link, have someone outside the original conversation check:

- Can they identify the question and find a complete answer without pressing play?
- Does the link land at the promised section, with working playback and readable text?
- Are the speaker's role, relevant date or version, and important limits visible?
- Does the example preserve the original meaning rather than promise the same result for everyone?
- Can the intended recipient open it, without exposing restricted content through the page or preview?
- Does the reply remain useful if the recipient ignores the link?

## When Not To Use

- No approved recording actually answers the question. Write a useful answer instead of forcing a media link.
- Permission is unclear, the material is stale, or nobody can maintain the destination.
- The placement interrupts the task through autoplay, intrusive overlays or a required content gate.
- The real aim is unsolicited prospecting rather than responding to an expressed need.

## Riggg Score

3 — Viable. Useful when a team has relevant, cleared assets and can maintain them; it still requires human selection and judgment. The score is not a claim of measured business lift.

## Evidence

Evidence level: external-research.

- **Reported adoption:** [Consensus's Bazaarvoice case](https://goconsensus.com/customer-story/accelerating-implementation-with-automated-demos/) describes a library of repeatable implementation and overview demos used before purchase. [Its Coupa case](https://goconsensus.com/customer-story/how-coupa-decreased-buying-cycle-times-by-29-with-consensus/) describes a reusable demo library and sharing within buyer groups. These are vendor-published accounts about demos, not proof of the interview-based examples above. Their advertised performance figures are not expected results for this practice.
- **Documented capability:** [Intercom's article-sharing guide](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/6564531-share-articles-to-answer-questions-faster) describes selecting articles or sections for customer replies. [Gong's search documentation](https://help.gong.io/docs/search-for-calls) describes transcript and title search with access requirements. These document functionality, not outcomes or a recommendation to buy either tool.

Source captures from October 6, 2026 were reviewed for this draft. The proposed applications and reporting example are illustrative; no pilot results are claimed.
